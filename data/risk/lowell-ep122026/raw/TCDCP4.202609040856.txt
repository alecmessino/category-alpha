Hurricane Lowell Discussion Number  32
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 PM HST Thu Sep 03 2026
 
Satellite images show that Lowell's eye has become less distinct 
during the past several hours, though very deep convection is 
ongoing in the eyewall.  The infrared presentation also suggests 
that Lowell has a double eyewall structure, which is confirmed by 
overnight microwave data.  The initial intensity is reduced to 110 
kt on this advisory, reflective of a small decrease in the overall 
Dvorak values and overall organization of the cloud pattern.  

Lowell still has plenty of time for re-strengthening since it will 
remain in a low-shear, warm-water environment for the next few 
days.  It should be noted that the wind shear has been higher 
than expected, especially in the mid-levels, which is possibly 
causing the sharp edge currently observed in the deep convection 
in the eastern eyewall.  However, this shear should abate by the 
weekend, and there is a long window of generally favorable 
conditions for the current eyewall replacement cycle to complete. 
Thus, the new NHC forecast continues to show some 
re-intensification, similar to the previous advisory and the 
guidance mean.  Around 72 h, Lowell is expected to encounter 
increasing southwesterly shear that should cause weakening sometime 
early next week. However, it is still forecast to be at major 
hurricane strength when makes its closest approach to the Hawaiian 
Islands.
 
There is no change to the initial motion of Lowell of 270/8 kt.  
The hurricane should gradually slow down during the next day or so 
due to a deepening mid- to upper-level trough north of the Hawaiian
Islands, which should cause a weaker subtropical ridge.  By late 
this weekend, the trough is forecast to break down the ridge, 
causing Lowell to move faster to the north or north-northeast late 
Sunday into Monday.  This track would bring the center of Lowell 
near or just west of the westernmost main Hawaiian Islands late 
Monday into early Tuesday.  The guidance envelope has made a 
westward shift on this advisory, seemingly due to a delayed 
northward turn.  The new NHC track forecast is nudged westward in 
line with the latest consensus and Google DeepMind guidance, but it 
should be noted that the long-range track details are still of 
lower confidence.
 
 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Lowell could be at or near major hurricane strength when it
moves near the westernmost main Hawaiian Islands late Monday and
Monday night. While it is still too soon to determine the exact
track of the hurricane, Lowell could bring storm surge, wind, and
rainfall impacts to portions of Hawaii early next week.
 
2. Interests in Hawaii should continue to closely monitor the
progress of Lowell through the upcoming Labor Day weekend, listen to
the advice of local officials, and ensure they have their hurricane
plan and supply kit in place.
 
3. Swells from Lowell are likely to cause life-threatening surf
and rip current conditions in portions of the Hawaiian Islands
during the next several days.  These swells are likely to increase
early next week.
