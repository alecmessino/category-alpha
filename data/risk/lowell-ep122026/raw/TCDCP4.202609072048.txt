Hurricane Lowell Discussion Number  47
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 AM HST Mon Sep 07 2026
  
The satellite presentation of Lowell has slightly degraded during
the past several hours, with the Air Force Hurricane Hunter showing
slowly rising central pressures.  There is also a tilt on recent
microwave imagery due to increasing southwesterly shear, and the
low-level center is a bit west of the ragged eye in conventional
satellite data.  Although the peak flight-level winds at 700 mb 
were 113 kt, the plane also reported the loss of an eyewall, which 
suggests the standard 90 percent rule will be too high for a 
surface wind reduction.  Thus, the initial wind speed is set to    
95 kt, which also is close to the most recent JTWC and HFO 
classifications. 
 
The initial motion is north-northeastward or 030/15 kt.  Lowell's
motion should gradually bend leftward due to the steering from a
mid- to upper-level low north of Hawaii and a building ridge to the
northeast.  The latest guidance is almost the same as before,
moving the center just west of Niihau tonight, and the new NHC
track in the short-term is only nudged slightly eastward.  After
passing the main Hawaiian Islands, Lowell will likely get wrapped
around the aforementioned upper-level low and take a westward turn
after 48 hours.  At long range, the system could turn northwestward
due to a new trough interaction, but hopefully it won't survive due
to its weakened state.  The new forecast is very similar to the
previous one, and south of the latest GFS/ECMWF models.
 
Lowell should slowly weaken today due to increasing shear and 
decreasing SSTs. A more rapid weakening should occur after it 
passes Hawaii overnight as shear gets quite high, and it moves 
over even cooler waters.  Model guidance is showing a much weaker 
system by midweek, and the new NHC forecast follows that trend.  
Beyond Wednesday, only a gradual loss of strength is forecast due 
to shear lessening while the system is over marginally warm waters. 
Lowell could be transitioning to a post-tropical cyclone at day 5, 
but the possible trough interaction is leading to a wide range of 
solutions from re-strengthening to a quick dissipation.  For now, 
the forecast will follow the guidance mean, and it should be 
mentioned this is a low-confidence long-range prediction.
 
 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Kauai County: The core of Lowell is forecast to pass near Niihau 
tonight and early Tuesday, and damaging hurricane conditions are 
expected during that time, with the highest winds atop mountains and 
on windward and leeward slopes. Large damaging waves will impact 
exposed portions of the coastline, producing dangerous coastal 
flooding and inundation of low-lying areas through early Tuesday. 

2. Periods of heavy rainfall will increase in coverage over the 
Hawaiian Islands today and continue through Wednesday. This 
may produce significant flash flooding and mudslides, especially 
across Kauai County and portions of the Big Island. 

3. Oahu: Tropical storm conditions are expected to begin tonight. 
Strong, damaging wind gusts could cause tree damage and power 
outages, especially in areas of higher terrain. 

4. Large swells will produce life-threatening surf and rip current 
conditions during the next several days across portions of the 
Hawaiian Islands.
