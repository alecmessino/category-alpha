Hurricane Lowell Discussion Number  45
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 PM HST Sun Sep 06 2026
 
In satellite imagery, Lowell remains well organized with a 
well-defined eye and cooling cloud tops in the eyewall. However, 
reports from an Air Force Reserve Hurricane Hunter aircraft show 
that the storm has multiple concentric wind maxima around the eye 
and that the central pressure has slowly risen to near 950 mb.  The 
maximum 700-mb flight-level winds reported by the plane were 107 kt 
east of the center. Based on the aircraft reports, the initial 
intensity is set at 100 kt.

The initial motion is now north-northeastward or 020/8 kt. The 
synoptic reasoning of the first part of the forecast track is 
unchanged from the previous advisory. A cutoff mid- to upper-level 
low sliding west-southwest north of Hawaii is beginning to capture 
Lowell, and this, combined with mid-level ridging to the 
hurricane's southeast should generally impart a faster north- 
northeastward to northward motion to the storm for the next 24-48 h. 
The track guidance envelope has shifted a little farther to the 
east, and the first 36 h or so of the new forecast track has been 
again nudged eastward. After 48 h, the cut-off low is forecast to 
weaken and lift out, with Lowell turning westward as a ridge builds 
to the north. This portion of the forecast track lies a little 
south of the previous forecast.

Lowell is starting to encounter increasing shear, and this, combined 
with gradually cooling sea surface temperatures along the forecast 
track, should cause weakening. However, Lowell is likely to remain a 
hurricane until after its closest approach to the Hawaiian Islands. 
After passing Hawaii, the intensity forecast becomes more uncertain, 
as the global models are in poor agreement on how the cyclone may 
interact with one or more new mid- to upper-level troughs that are 
forecast to form nearby. For now, this part of the intensity 
forecast is little changed from the previous forecast.
 
Given another eastward shift in the track guidance and the forecast 
close approach of hurricane-force winds to Niihau, a Hurricane 
Warning has been issued for Kauai County.
 
 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Lowell's center is forecast to pass just west of Kauai County
late Monday into early Tuesday. A Hurricane Warning is now in 
effect for Kauai County, including Kauai and Niihau, where 
hurricane conditions are possible are expected to begin Monday 
afternoon and continue through Tuesday morning. Enhanced wind gusts 
are possible atop mountains and on windward and leeward slopes.
 
2. Periods of heavy rainfall associated with Hurricane Lowell will 
increase in coverage over the Hawaiian Islands tonight and continue 
through Wednesday. This may produce significant flash flooding and 
mudslides, especially across Kauai county and portions of the Big 
Island.
 
3. Large swells from Lowell are expected to produce life-threatening
surf and rip current conditions across portions of the Hawaiian
Islands during the next several days. Surf along exposed portions
of Kauai County could produce coastal flooding and inundation of
low-lying areas Monday through Monday night.
