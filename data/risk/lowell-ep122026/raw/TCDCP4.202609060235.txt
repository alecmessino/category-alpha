Hurricane Lowell Discussion Number  40
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
500 PM HST Sat Sep 05 2026
 
Lowell remains an impressive and well-organized major hurricane this 
afternoon, although satellite imagery suggests that some weakening 
has occurred during the past several hours. The eye remains well 
defined and is surrounded by a largely symmetric eyewall, but 
visible and infrared imagery show some erosion of the convection 
within the northeastern portion of the eyewall, where cloud-top 
temperatures have warmed. This could be the result of some mid-level 
dry air becoming entrained into the inner core. Although an eyewall 
replacement cycle cannot be ruled out, there are still no recent 
microwave data to indicate that a secondary eyewall has formed. 
Subjective Dvorak Current Intensity estimates from HFO and JTWC are 
both 6.0, corresponding to 115 kt, while objective intensity 
estimates range from about 105 to 125 kt. Based on a blend of these 
data and the modest degradation of the satellite presentation, the 
initial intensity is lowered to 115 kt for this advisory. A NOAA 
G-IV aircraft is currently sampling the environment surrounding 
Lowell, and these data should be incorporated into upcoming model 
cycles.
 
Lowell has continued to slowly gain latitude, with an initial motion 
of 300/4 kt. A cutoff upper-level low north-northeast of the 
Hawaiian Islands is forecast to move southwestward during the next 
couple of days. As Lowell becomes increasingly influenced by this 
feature, the hurricane should turn northward on Sunday and then 
north-northeastward while accelerating Sunday night and Monday. The 
track guidance remains in good agreement and continues to show the 
center of Lowell passing west of Kauai County late Monday into early 
Tuesday. The latest NHC track forecast is essentially an update of 
the previous advisory and brings the center of Lowell about 150 to 
175 n mi west of Niihau around the time of closest approach. After 
passing the main Hawaiian Islands, Lowell is forecast to turn 
west-northwestward and slow as weak ridging builds to the north of 
the cyclone.
 
Some fluctuations in intensity remain possible through early Sunday 
due to internal structural changes. However, the overall trend is 
expected to be toward gradual weakening. Deep-layer vertical wind 
shear is forecast to increase substantially beginning Sunday as 
Lowell turns northward and accelerates toward the vicinity of the 
western main Hawaiian Islands. Increasingly dry mid-level air should 
also become entrained into the circulation during this time. These 
increasingly hostile environmental conditions should cause the rate 
of weakening to increase Sunday night and Monday. Despite the 
expected weakening, Lowell is forecast to remain a major hurricane 
when it makes its closest approach to Kauai County and passes across 
portions of the Papahanaumokuakea Marine National Monument. 
Thereafter, a combination of strong vertical wind shear and drier 
air should result in continued weakening. The latest NHC intensity 
forecast is essentially an update of the previous advisory.
 
 
KEY MESSAGES:

1. Lowell is forecast to pass west of Kauai County late Monday into
early Tuesday. A Tropical Storm Watch has been issued for Kauai
County, including Kauai and Niihau, where tropical storm conditions
are possible beginning Monday. A Hurricane Watch has been issued
for portions of the Papahanaumokuakea Marine National Monument from
Nihoa to French Frigate Shoals.

2. Heavy rainfall associated with Hurricane Lowell will begin to 
impact the Hawaiian Islands Sunday afternoon and continue through 
early next week. Periods of heavy rain may produce significant flash 
flooding and mudslides, especially on Kauai and portions of the Big 
Island.

3. Large swells from Lowell are expected to produce life-threatening
surf and rip current conditions across portions of the Hawaiian
Islands during the next several days. Surf along exposed portions
of Kauai County could also produce coastal flooding and inundation
of low-lying areas late Monday into Monday night.
