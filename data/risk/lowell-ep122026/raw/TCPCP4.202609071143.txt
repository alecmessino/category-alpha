DISCUSSION AND OUTLOOK
----------------------
At 200 AM HST (1200 UTC), the center of Hurricane Lowell was located
near latitude 17.0 North, longitude 163.0 West. Lowell is moving
toward the north-northeast near 10 mph (17 km/h). A north- 
northeastward to northward motion at a faster forward speed is
forecast through Tuesday. On the forecast track, the center of
Lowell will move across portions of the Papahanaumokuakea Marine
National Monument, just west of the main Hawaiian Islands, tonight. 
Lowell is then forecast to turn toward the northwest and slow down 
by early Wednesday after passing the Hawaiian Islands.
 
Maximum sustained winds remain near 115 mph (185 km/h) with higher 
gusts. Lowell is a category 3 hurricane on the Saffir-Simpson 
Hurricane Wind Scale.  Gradual weakening is expected through 
Tuesday. However, Lowell is forecast to remain a hurricane as it 
passes near the Hawaiian Islands.
 
Hurricane-force winds extend outward up to 60 miles (95 km) from
the center and tropical-storm-force winds extend outward up to 175
miles (280 km).
 
The estimated minimum central pressure is 950 mb (28.06 inches).
 
 
HAZARDS AFFECTING LAND
----------------------
Key messages for Lowell can be found in the Tropical Cyclone
Discussion under AWIPS header HFOTCDCP4 and WMO header WTPA44
PHFO.
 
WIND: Hurricane conditions are expected in Kauai County beginning 
this afternoon or evening. Wind speeds atop and on the windward and 
leeward sides of hills and mountains are often up to 30 percent 
stronger than the near-surface winds.
 
Hurricane conditions are expected in the Papahanaumokuakea Marine
National Monument between Nihoa and French Frigate Shoals tonight, 
with tropical storm conditions beginning this afternoon.
 
RAINFALL: Hurricane Lowell is expected to produce additional
rainfall totals of 6 to 10 inches for Kauai, with maximum totals of
14 inches possible. For the Big Island, 4 to 8 inches of rain is
expected with isolated totals of 12 inches possible. For Oahu, 4 to
6 inches of rain is expected with isolated totals of 8 inches
possible.  For Maui, 2 to 4 inches of rain is expected, with
isolated maximum totals of 8 inches possible. Across the remaining
islands, rainfall of 2 to 4 inches is possible. This rainfall may
produce significant flash flooding and mudslides, especially across
Kauai county and portions of the Big Island.
 
For a complete depiction of forecast rainfall associated with
Hurricane Lowell, please see the National Weather Service Storm
Total Rainfall Graphic available at
hurricanes.gov/graphics_ep12.shtml?rainqpf
 
STORM SURGE: The combination of storm surge and tide will cause
normally dry areas near the coast to be flooded by rising waters
moving inland from the shoreline. The water could reach the
following heights above ground somewhere in the indicated areas if
the peak surge occurs at the time of high tide...
 
Kauai County...1-3 ft
Oahu...Up to 1 ft
 
The deepest water will occur along the immediate coast near and to
the right of the center, where the surge will be accompanied by
large and destructive waves. Surge-related flooding depends on the
relative timing of the surge and the tidal cycle, and can vary
greatly over short distances.
 
For a complete depiction of areas at risk of storm surge inundation,
please see the National Weather Service Peak Storm Surge Graphic,
available at hurricanes.gov/graphics_cp4.shtml?peakSurge.
 
TORNADOES: Several tornadoes are possible near Kauai and Niihau
this afternoon through tonight. A few tornadoes are also possible 
across the remainder of the Hawaiian Islands.
 
SURF: Swells from Lowell are likely to cause life-threatening surf
and rip current conditions during the next several days across
many of the Hawaiian islands. Please consult products from your
local weather office.
