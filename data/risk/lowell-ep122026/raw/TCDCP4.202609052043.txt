Hurricane Lowell Discussion Number  39
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   EP122026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
1100 AM HST Sat Sep 05 2026
 
Lowell's satellite presentation has degraded further this morning 
with what appears to be a moat developing around the eyewall in 
visible and satellite imagery. This could suggest that Lowell is 
beginning to go through an eyewall replacement, but the most recent 
microwave data did not show signs of a secondary eyewall. It's also 
possible that the hurricane has ingested dry air that is eroding 
some of the convection outside of the inner core. Either way, 
subjective Dvorak Current Intensity numbers and objective estimates 
now support an initial intensity of 125 kt.

Lowell appears to have begun its expected turn, with the initial 
motion now west-northwestward (285/6 kt). A deep-layer low located 
about 500 n mi north-northeast of the Hawaiian Islands is forecast 
to dip southwestward over the next couple of days, which will cause 
Lowell to make a sharp northward to north-northeastward turn, 
accompanied by an increase in forward speed, by Sunday and Monday. 
The track guidance has not changed much since the last cycle and 
remains in good agreement that Lowell's center will move 100-200 n 
mi to the west of Kauai County in 60-72 hours. Since the models 
didn't change much, the new NHC track forecast is very similar to 
the previous forecast. After passing the main Hawaiian Islands after 
day 3, Lowell is forecast to turn toward the west-northwest and slow 
down as weak ridging again builds in north of the cyclone.
 
The official intensity forecast shows a steady intensity for the 
next 12 hours or so, leaving the door open for possible 
fluctuations in intensity, especially if an eyewall replacement 
does occur. Overall, however, models suggest that gradual weakening 
will continue, especially once deep-layer shear increases in 36-48 
hours. The updated NHC intensity forecast has again been lowered 
slightly from the previous prediction to follow model trends, but 
it still shows Lowell being near or at major hurricane strength when 
it makes its closest approach to the western main Hawaiian Islands 
and passes across portions of the Papahanaumokuakea Marine National 
Monument. After day 3, the addition of cooler waters and drier air 
should cause Lowell to weaken to a tropical storm by day 4.
 
 
KEY MESSAGES:
 
1. Lowell is forecast to move close to the western Hawaiian Islands
late Monday and Monday night. There is a risk of wind and coastal 
flooding impacts for Kauai County at that time, and a tropical 
storm watch will likely be issued later today. Residents and 
tourists should follow advice from local officials.
 
2. Heavy rainfall associated with Lowell will begin to impact the 
Hawaiian Islands Sunday afternoon and continue through early next 
week. Periods of heavy rain may produce locally significant flash 
flooding and mudslides, especially in areas of steep terrain.
 
3. Swells from Lowell are likely to cause life-threatening surf
and rip current conditions in portions of the Hawaiian Islands
during the next several days. These swells are likely to increase
early next week.
