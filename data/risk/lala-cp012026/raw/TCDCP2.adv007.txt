Tropical Storm Lala Discussion Number   7
NWS Central Pacific Hurricane Center Honolulu HI   CP012026
Issued by NWS National Hurricane Center Miami FL
500 PM HST Thu Aug 13 2026
 
Lala continues to steadily intensify. While the associated central 
convection has changed little over the past several hours, aircraft 
data show that the peak 700 mb flight-level winds have increased to 
61 kt, and the pressure has dropped to 997 mb, although with a 
surface dropsonde wind of 49 kt. Based on these data, the initial 
intensity is raised to 50 kt for this advisory.
 
The initial motion estimate is west-northwestward, or 295/10 kt. 
The overall track forecast philosophy remains largely unchanged. A 
strong mid-level ridge north of the Hawaiian Islands should continue 
to steer the cyclone west-northwestward during the next several 
days. The cyclone's forward motion is forecast to slow somewhat as 
it approaches and begins to interact with the terrain of the Big 
Island on Saturday. Some short-term deviations in the track are 
also possible during this time due to these terrain interactions. 
Lala should then resume a faster west-northwestward motion as it 
passes south of the smaller islands through the remainder of the 
weekend, and south of the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands Sunday night 
into early next week. The latest NHC track forecast is very similar 
to the previous advisory, except for a slight northward adjustment 
in the lee of the Big Island, and lies close to the multi-model 
consensus (HCCA) and Google DeepMind ensemble mean. 

Environmental conditions are expected to remain generally favorable 
for strengthening during the next couple of days, with the primary 
limiting factor being the dry mid-level air that continues to be 
entrained into the cyclone's large circulation. Steady 
strengthening is forecast, and Lala is expected to become a 
hurricane as it approaches the Big Island on Saturday.  Interaction 
with the terrain of the Big Island could result in short-term 
changes in the cyclone's structure and intensity during its closest 
approach. Thereafter, an upper-level low is forecast to produce 
increasing vertical wind shear with increasing dry air continuing to 
entrain into the circulation as Lala passes south of the smaller 
islands, which should result in some weakening through the remainder 
of the weekend. Re-strengthening is forecast early next week as the 
cyclone moves away from the Hawaiian Islands and into a more 
favorable environment. The latest NHC intensity forecast is similar 
to the previous advisory and remains near the middle to upper end of 
the guidance envelope.
 
 
Key Messages:
 
1. Lala is forecast to strengthen and become a hurricane as it 
approaches the Big Island of Hawaii by early Saturday. A Hurricane 
Warning is now in effect for the Big Island and a Tropical Storm 
Watch is in effect for Maui County. Winds are expected to be the 
strongest in areas of higher terrain.
 
2. Heavy rainfall associated with Tropical Storm Lala will begin to
impact the Big Island Friday, spreading westward across the
remainder of the island chain during the weekend. This rainfall may
produce life-threatening flooding and mudslides, especially in areas
of steep terrain.
 
3. Large and dangerous waves, especially on the Big Island, are
expected this weekend, with the greatest impacts along exposed 
windward and southern shores. This prolonged period of heavy surf 
may cause localized coastal flooding and significant beach erosion.
 
4. Interests elsewhere in Hawaii should monitor the progress of
Lala, and Tropical Storm Watches or Warnings could be required on
Friday.
