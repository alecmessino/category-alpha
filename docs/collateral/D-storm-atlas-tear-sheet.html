Storm Atlas

Storm Atlas is a research-only historical genesis/outcome engine for the North Atlantic and East Pacific. Give it an observed genesis point or a declared pre-genesis reference cell; it returns what the record did next as exact n/N with a 95% Wilson interval, names every storm in the denominator, and refuses the rate where the record cannot carry one.

What a user can do

  • Condition on a point. An observed genesis position, or a declared pre-genesis reference cell, with a stated radius and month window. The season floor is a declared boundary, not a preference: an observing-bias gap before 1971 is why the floor sits at 1971, and a cohort reaching behind it is warned rather than pooled silently. Rates are genesis-conditioned and assume formation — not P(forms), not a live feed, not a forecast cone.
  • Retrieve the analog tracks. Every storm in the cohort, with representative members printed under a stated rule — name, season, genesis date, distance from the cell, lifetime peak, and its own landfalls as recorded. Storm Atlas implements no similarity metric, so nothing is ranked as a closest or best analog.
  • Compare contract-row frequencies. Reached TD, TS and Cat 1 through Cat 5, each as exact n/N with its 95% Wilson interval. Time-to-threshold medians with p25 and p75 sit alongside and carry their own n: the min-sample gate governs rates, not medians, so a median can be printed on an n of one or two. Read the n before the median.
  • Inspect the landfall contract rows. conus, mexico, caribbean, central_america, hawaii and unattributed, each with an "any" and a ≥64 kt pair — but which of the twelve score is a property of the cohort, not of the archive: the Karina panel scores the mexico pair and refuses the other ten. State-level and sub-CONUS landfall is not scored at all. Sub-region is carried in the archive and can be read off a named storm's own landfall record; it is not a contract row, and no state-level rate exists.
  • Read the refusals as first-class output. Three gates, all printed. A cohort below min sample publishes counts and no rates at all — no rate column, no interval. A contract out of scope for the basin, or an outcome too rare archive-wide to score, prints its status beside a figure the status voids: where the status column is set, the 0.0% and the interval on that row are not results and must not be read as a zero. Hawaii ≥64 kt is base rate only — 2 archive-wide against the 10 required — and is never a scored probability.
  • Carry it into the work. Set a candidate intensity or landfall threshold and count the cohort members that cross it; they are named, so the count is checkable. Stress the cell before quoting anything — move it, and watch N and the refusal state move with it. Hand a reviewer the URL instead of a number. Environment — shear, mid-level RH, SST, OHC — is displayed against the cohort you already chose; it never changes who is in it.

THE MOAT — SANITATION, REFUSAL, PROVENANCE

An interpolated fix is never allowed to establish a threshold crossing, so time-to-hurricane is never dated off a wind nobody measured. A segment crossing publishes no Saffir-Simpson class where the two bracketing fixes disagree on it — the interpolated wind stays on the row, labelled by detection method; the class derived from it does not. A cohort's defining variable is not reported back as a scored outcome, with one exception left in the open: every cohort returns reached TD at 100%, because a cohort is storms that formed. That row is definitional and carries no information; the panels here start at reached TS. Below min sample a rate is refused rather than estimated — the same 97L cell read at 150 km instead of 250 km returns 5 storms against a min sample of 10, and every rate on that panel reads RATE REFUSED. A cohort reaching behind 1971 carries the observing-bias warning instead of pooling the record silently. An absent outcome is not a zero: an unrecorded intensity leaves the denominator and is counted on its own, so an n/N row can carry a smaller N than its cohort — no sample on this sheet exercises it, and every row runs on 12, on 14 and on 6. Every figure, refusals included, carries METHODOLOGY 1.1.0, PACK 134661125525f27a and a URL that reproduces it.

03

Two worked samples

97L and Karina · every figure replayable

Three cohorts follow, printed as the engine returned them. The cell is an input, not an observation: 97L is read at a desk-declared pre-genesis reference cell, 28.0°N 88.7°W — not the disturbance's current position, not the outlook area. Storm Atlas does not choose the cell and does not defend it; the URL records what was chosen, so a reviewer can move it and watch the answer move. Cohort N 12 at 250 km; the same cell read at 150 km returns 5, below the min sample of 10, every rate refused — which is why the radius is printed with the number. Karina is read at its observed genesis, 13.2°N 115.0°W, a datum rather than a choice, cohort N 14. It is the harder sample and it is here for that reason: the cohort returns reached Cat 4 at 1/14 [1–31%] and reached Cat 5 at 0/14 [0–22%], and Karina is already Cat 4. That is what a genesis-conditioned rate is — the record's frequency at the moment of formation. The cohort does not know Karina intensified and will not be revised because it did. Sample 3 is TS Lowell, read at its observed genesis 11.3°N 133.8°W: cohort N 6, below min sample, counts published and every rate refused — including the Hawaii rows, 1 of 6, where the one is Iniki 1992. A six-storm Hawaii rate is the number a desk would most want and the one the engine will not print. Scored rows carry exact n/N and the 95% Wilson interval; a status voids the figure beside it; a cohort below min sample returns no rate and no interval at all. Every figure replays from the URL beneath its table.

04

Delivery

CURRENT — SHIPPING TODAY

Delivery today is a browser and replayable cohort URLs. A URL carries the cell, radius, season floor, month window, basin and methodology version, so a colleague, a client or a reviewer opens the same cohort and reads the same counts, the same rates and intervals where the engine scored them, and the same refusals where it did not. It reproduces under the same pack stamp: the pack is a hash of every table it was built from, so a rebuilt archive changes the stamp, and a figure whose stamp does not match is not the same figure.

PROPOSED / PILOT — NOT SHIPPING

PROPOSED / PILOT — not available today. Exportable charts. An API. Recurring institutional briefs. None of the three exists yet.

What Storm Atlas adds

The question a desk actually asks NHC / Zoom Earth / public maps Storm Atlas
Where is it now, and how strong?The observation: position, intensity, pressure, motion — now.Silent. Storm Atlas holds no live view and makes none.
Where will it go?The forecast cone and official track guidance, 5 days.Silent, by construction. No cone, no track forecast, no skill claim.
Will it form at all?P(forms) — the graphical outlook's 48 h and 7 d chance.Silent. Every rate here is conditional on formation and says nothing about it.
What happened to storms that began here before?Not answered. No cohort, no denominator, no interval.Exact n / N with a 95% Wilson interval on every contract row, from a declared genesis point or pre-genesis reference cell, with the analog tracks drawn.
How good is the evidence, where does it run out, and can a counterparty reproduce it?Not answered. The advisory is public; the reasoning behind a number is not reproducible from it.Visible refusal — effective sample size, the min-sample gate, the archive-wide event gate and the pre-satellite bias warning, printed beside the number or instead of it. And the cohort is a URL: same question, same pack stamp, same numbers, by anyone.

RESEARCH ONLY — NOT A FORECAST. Storm Atlas is a historical genesis/outcome engine. Every rate on this page is GENESIS-CONDITIONED: it assumes a tropical cyclone forms and describes what the historical record did next. It is not P(forms), not a live feed, and not a forecast cone. To combine with a formation probability: P(reaches X) = P(forms) × P(reaches X | forms). Landfall does not decompose that way and is counted jointly, never as a product of marginals.
