The 2024 fact sheet states that payouts are triggered and calculated from officially reported wind speeds and proximity to the core zone. The 2026 zone geometry, payout schedule, designated observation source and wind definition are not published, so neither the amount nor the difference from Lowell's $300,000 can be traced to a condition. Millibar does not infer an undisclosed contract term from an observed payout. Supplied with the schedule, the same evaluator resolves this record deterministically.

It arrived at 18.3N 155.7W, 36 nm out — behind and to the left of the forecast track, which is to say further from the island than forecast, not closer. Two things this archive cannot do that the Lowell archive could

Both records are built by the same evaluator from the same kind of products. They were not acquired the same way, and the difference is carried rather than smoothed over.

Issuance time is known to the hour, not the minute

The Lowell products were captured from the WMO feed as they went out, so each filename carries a transmission time to the minute — a 15Z advisory transmitted at 14:51Z. These products come from NHC's public archive, which replaces the transmission group with the literal TTAA00 PHFO DDHHMM. The minute is not in these bytes and is not reconstructed. Every issuance time on this page is the hour the product prints for itself.

Where a public advisory prints only a local stamp, the UTC instant is derived from the fixed HST offset and then required to reproduce the UTC hour the product states in its own summary line. Eleven PM HST on the 16th is 09Z on the 17th, and a record that got the date from the wrong line would be wrong by a day rather than by an hour. Disagreement is refused, not resolved.

No ICAO aviation advisory is archived

The Lowell record's headline residual is measured against NHC's own interpolated position for an off-cycle hour, taken from the aviation advisory issued alongside Advisory 46. No aviation product for Lala is held in the public archive, so that construction is unavailable here. This record does not substitute an interpolation of its own: it reports at a synoptic hour, where official forecast rows actually exist.

Three cycles proven from the discussion, not the advisory

A forecast/advisory's nominal cycle is normally recoverable from its own rows, because they land on the canonical lead set and on nothing else. Advisories 62, 63, 64 print only five rows as the system dissipates, and a five-row set beginning at +12 h is equally canonical read as +24 h from a cycle six hours earlier. The evaluator refuses that ambiguity rather than resolving it by convention, and takes the cycle from the companion discussion, which prints the lead label outright.

Neither limitation touches a figure on this page: the island passage is covered by forecast/advisories, public advisories and off-schedule updates, all of which state their own times, and the residual is reported at a synoptic hour against official rows.
