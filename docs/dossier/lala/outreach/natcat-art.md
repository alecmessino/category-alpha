# Millibar — NatCat research and alternative risk transfer

*Audience: NatCat research / ART structuring. No claim is made about any firm's book.*

Lala (CP012026, 2026) is the worked example. The archive record every model validates against holds 49 fixes, 65 kt, 988 mb, ending 2026-08-16 00:00Z. The forecast office's own record holds 64 fixes, 115 kt, 947 mb, and was still open at 2026-08-25 18:00Z. Millibar preserves both, labels both, and blends neither.

What the dossier demonstrates that a vendor view usually does not:

- **The record refuses.** Around Lala's genesis point the cohort is 26 storms
  (effective sample size 26). Hawaiʻi landfall is
  1/26 — and the entire numerator is one storm,
  Dot (1959). The archive then states its own defect: 8
  of the 26 predate 1971, when major hurricanes were
  under-observed. Apply its recommended remedy and the cohort falls to 18, the numerator
  goes to zero, and the rate is withheld: **OUT OF SCOPE -- unscoreable here**. The system publishes the
  refusal rather than the 3.8%.
- **Provisional and post-analysed records are kept apart.** Lala is excluded from its own
  historical cohort by the archive's provisional scope, so no live value reaches any statistic.
  That exclusion is enforced by a build gate, not by convention.
- **Every rate carries its interval and its sample.** 15.4% reached
  Category 4 or higher, interval 6.1–33.5%, 4 of
  26.

Useful as an independent reference to challenge an in-house or vendor view against — including
where it declines to answer.
