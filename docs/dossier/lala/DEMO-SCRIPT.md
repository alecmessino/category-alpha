Timings are cumulative. Every number below is on screen — do not quote one that is not.

## 0:25 – 1:00 · Where the archive stops

**Screen:** `/dossier/lala`, section 2, the chronology.

> "Watch the distance column. Lala starts 1,942 km from the
> main Hawaiian islands and closes. The archive record ends here —" **[point: 2026-08-16 00:00Z]** —

## 1:00 – 1:30 · The interpretation to reject

> "Lala peaked at 115 knots. It would be easy to sell that as a
> Category 4 near-miss of Hawaiʻi. It was not. The peak was
> 3.0 d later and
> 946 km from those islands — 15 times
> the closest approach. Intensity and proximity did not coincide. That separation is the thing
> worth studying."

## 1:30 – 2:20 · What the record will and will not support

**Screen:** section 3.

> "Now the historical question: what happened to storms that formed where Lala formed?
> 26 storms, effective sample size 26, every rate with a Wilson
> interval. 15.4% reached Category 4 or higher — interval
> 6.1–33.5%."

> "Hawaiʻi landfall: 1 of 26. One storm —
> Dot, 1959, Kauai. The archive then tells us
> its own data is not good enough:" **[point: the gap statement]** "8 of these
> 26 storms predate 1971, when major hurricanes were
> under-observed. Apply the archive's own remedy —" **[point: the 1971
> column]** "— and the cohort drops to 18, Dot drops out, and the
> Hawaiʻi rate stops existing: **OUT OF SCOPE -- unscoreable here**."

> "Most systems would have shown you 3.8%. This one shows you why that
> number should not be used."

**Also point at:** *Lala is not in this cohort* — it is excluded by the archive's own provisional
scope, so no live data touches the statistics.

## 2:20 – 2:50 · Point-in-time, not hindsight

> "And the calibration ledger refuses to score it: 2 resolved
