How to read every number on this page

Four sources answer four different questions. They are labelled everywhere and never combined.

ARCHIVEIBTrACS best track, as the Storm Atlas holds it. Post-analysed for closed seasons; provisional for this one. The only source any statistic on this page is computed from.
OPERATIONALATCF b-deck and operational SHIPS, as the forecast office had them. Revised while a storm is live. Used to describe this storm, and nothing else.
DERIVEDComputed here by replaying a rule the archive already owns. The rule is named wherever a derived value appears.
RECORDED / MILLIBARTimestamped Millibar system output, pinned here from docs/data/forecast-log.json. Evidence of what the system recorded at that instant. It is not an upstream observation, it is not evidence of forecasting skill, and it was not necessarily published externally at the time.

No external or public contract fact is used anywhere in this document. No carrier, policy, trigger geometry, attachment threshold or payout function is named, reconstructed or implied, and no price quoted or settled outside Millibar enters any figure here.

The entire Hawaiʻi numerator is one storm

Dot (1959), CP011959 — Kauai, 1959-08-07, 75 kt, Category 1, detection bracketing_fix. A rate of 3.8% whose numerator is a single 1959 event is a count, not a frequency, and its interval 0.7–18.9% says so.

Under the archive's own quality remedy, the Hawaiʻi rate refuses

8 of 26 storms in this cohort are from before 1971, when East Pacific intensities were estimated without geostationary satellites or Dvorak analysis and major hurricanes were under-observed (measured: 1.7% Cat 3 in the 1960s vs 20-30% from the 1970s on). Intensity rates above are therefore biased LOW. Set a season floor of 1971 to restrict the cohort to the reliably-observed era.

Applying that remedy — a season floor of 1971 — takes the cohort from 26 storms to 18, and removes Dot (1959) with it. The Hawaiʻi landfall outcome becomes 0/18 and the engine stops publishing a rate: OUT OF SCOPE -- unscoreable here.

This is the answer to what the historical record supports about Hawaiʻi risk at Lala's genesis point: over the reliably-observed era, it declines to support a rate at all.

This demonstrates replay discipline, not forecasting skill

Millibar's calibration ledger declines to publish a score: 2 resolved storms of the 10 required, behind 59 resolved forecasts.

2 resolved storms of the 10 needed to publish a score. 59 resolved forecasts sit behind them, and that gap is the reason the threshold counts STORMS: every forecast made during one storm's life shares that storm's single outcome, so scoring the entries would quote a handful of coin flips to three decimal places.

One storm cannot establish calibration, and this document does not claim it does. What the ledger establishes is that the values above were recorded before the outcome and have not been edited since — which is a provenance property, and a precondition for measuring skill later rather than a substitute for having measured it.

5 Provenance discipline, demonstrated

Lala is a case where two authoritative sources disagreed by 50 kt and 41 mb, and both were kept.

The archive record is not extended, corrected or overwritten by the operational one. IBTrACS publishes a provisional row for a running season and revises it at post-analysis; that row is the input to every historical statistic Millibar computes, and it stays exactly as published. The operational deck describes the storm that is happening. Both are on this page, labelled, with their own timestamps and their own source files.

The separation is enforced rather than observed. The operational layer cannot be reached by any module that computes cohort membership, analog matching, intensity or landfall rates, Wilson intervals, effective sample size, calibration, the archive comparison, an event gate or a refusal — a build gate walks the import graph and fails when one can, and a second gate recomputes every published historical value with the operational artifact absent and again with it loaded, requiring them to be identical.

The two records also agree where they overlap, which is worth stating: both begin at 2026-08-10 00:00Z, and both place genesis at 2026-08-13 12:00Z, 14.9°N 145.0°W. The disagreement is entirely about where the record stops.
